Abstract

This ontology provides a structured framework for the interoperability of health economics data used to create models. The ontology represents all the concepts and relationships involved in health technology assessment, including the characterization of the disease and its development, the population affected, and the interventions applied.

Acute manifestationc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/ontologies-ULL/OSDi#AcuteManifestation

Sudden or rapid appearance of clinical signs or symptoms, in general with a short and intense course. Its costs and utilities apply per episode, so they should be one-time (appliesOneTime = true); a one-time utility is a loss of QALYs. In a decision tree, an acute manifestation represents a single episode between the initial age of the population and death: its risk characterization is the probability that the episode happens, and, as for any other progression, an acute manifestation without risk always happens in its context. A decision tree ignores the frequency (hasFrequency) of an acute manifestation that defines a risk, and cannot represent one that only defines a frequency. Annual costs and utilities of an acute manifestation are ignored both in decision trees and in discrete-event simulations.

Adverse eventc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/ontologies-ULL/OSDi#AdverseEvent

A health condition caused by a strategy (hasAdverseEvent), e.g., the diarrhoea caused by an antibiotic. Each time a strategy is applied, each of its adverse events happens with its risk (hasRiskCharacterization: a probability per application, or a time to event in a simulation), independently of the outcome of the strategy, unless the strategy fails because of the adverse event (failsIf), e.g., when the treatment is abandoned. Its costs and utilities apply once, when it happens (annual ones are not meaningful); its effects (including an InstantDeathEffect) and its own strategies apply to the patients who suffer it. isOccurrenceOf identifies the health condition it represents, but does not activate the structure of that condition. Rule for the modeller: if the problem belongs to the natural history of the disease and the strategy only changes its risk, use an Effect of the strategy on that risk; if the problem is caused by the strategy, use an AdverseEvent.

Effectc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/ontologies-ULL/OSDi#Effect

An effect of a treatment or intervention. It applies its magnitude (hasEffectMagnitude) to the parameters or attributes it affects (affects). The subclasses define how the magnitude is applied (an instant death effect sets a probability of death, a life expectancy reduction effect subtracts years of life and an increased mortality rate effect multiplies the mortality rate), and a strategy replacement effect replaces the strategies of a health condition (see StrategyReplacementEffect); for a generic effect, the data item type of the magnitude defines the operation (see hasEffectMagnitude). When several effects modify the same parameter, the order is decided by the application; the suggested order is: first replace, then multiply (linear factors, then ratios of rates, then odds ratios) and finally subtract. At most one effect should replace the same parameter.

Methods suffixed with _DISTINCT count only once a parameter individual that is shared by several simultaneously active items (e.g., the same follow-up visit required by two manifestations). ADD sums every reduction; ADD_DISTINCT sums them, but counts only once each magnitude parameter; MAX applies only the largest reduction. If not defined, MAX is assumed. Increased mortality rates (see hasMortalityRateCombinationMethod) are applied first, by dividing the life expectancy by the combined rate; then the combined life expectancy reduction is subtracted.

Experiment c
